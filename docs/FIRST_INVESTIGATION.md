# First Investigation — v1.8

## Design boundary

The authoritative world generates an incident when conditions permit. A player-visible case is a **report**, not direct access to world truth. Engine data retains `offender_id` for deterministic validation, but no detective command lists that identity.

## Incident logic

- For this first prototype only, the world samples opportunities at 17:30 on one evening of each seven-day game week.
- An incident requires at least three living, awake, co-present residents at a shared public scene (park, cafe or market).
- The potential offender is selected from those present using personal carefulness, resources and a stable personal seed. The victim is another present resident with sufficient cash.
- A missing wallet transfers 15 in-game euros from victim to offender and produces an authoritative incident event whose **text does not identify the offender**.
- Residents at the precise scene are recorded as possible witnesses. A later arrival is not a witness.
- Cases are generated by game rules, not by OpenAI or a fixed list of plot characters.

## Detective commands (English only)

```text
cases
case case-0001
inspect case-0001
interview resident-0001 case-0001
interview marco case-0001
accuse case-0001 resident-0003
```

- `cases`: public reports only.
- `case`: a public summary plus collected clues/interviews.
- `inspect`: inspect the scene; adds a tentative clue that needs corroboration.
- `interview`: record one per-resident attributed account, based on possible eyewitness status and hearsay.
- `accuse`: requires scene inspection and at least two interviews; compares the accusation to simulator-held facts. A wrong accusation leaves the case open without naming the culprit.

## SQLite

Additive tables: `investigation_cases`, `investigation_witnesses`, and `investigation_interviews`. The historical v1.7 tables and world database are not reset. Cases and their current state persist across restarts.

## Current limitations

The theft motive is an early-stage opportunity heuristic, not a psychological crime model. Only a wallet can go missing. There is no actual player body, travel or action duration; interviews are instant, text is templated, and a witness's report cannot by itself prove guilt. Users can still inspect `town-events` / `secrets` developer diagnostics, which spoil investigative play. The accusation check uses omniscient engine truth internally and should be replaced later by a corroborated-evidence adjudication system.
