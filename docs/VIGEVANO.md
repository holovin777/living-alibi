# Vigevano geographic preview

Run from the repository root:

    bash play.sh --map

First launch requests one central Vigevano extract from the public Overpass API, then caches the OSM XML and converted JSON in data/. A saved XML file can be imported offline:

    python3 tools/import_vigevano.py --input data/vigevano.osm --output data/vigevano.json

Use --refresh only if map data needs updating. Do not repeatedly download. The bounding box is 45.3100,8.8480,45.3240,8.8700 (roughly 1.56 x 1.72 km), an initial central extract, not the municipality. Coordinates are locally projected into meters around Piazza Ducale. Building footprints become extruded walls and triangulated roofs; missing heights are estimated; road segments and rail traces are drawn from source coordinates. Geometry is streamed in a 3x3 window of 128 m chunks. Walk with WASD, click to capture mouse, Escape release, Space jump. This is an independent preview, not yet linked to Java simulation, vehicle routing, the SQLite world or NPCs. Original bash play.sh is unchanged. Building relations, courtyards, exact facades, slope, bridges/tunnels and real landmark models are not implemented. Street names are retained in data but not yet displayed. Map data are © OpenStreetMap contributors, licensed ODbL: https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright — preserve attribution and ODbL obligations when distributing derived map databases. The test fixture is synthetic and not actual map data.

Schema 2 adds a road graph keyed by real OSM node IDs. Existing XML is re-converted automatically (no redownload). The M key opens a clickable full-extract map; select a street to calculate a walking path, and the HUD shows the nearest named road and route distance. The graph also represents basic motor access and directed one-way roads, but driving is not yet connected. Routing uses nodes and straight segments; turn restrictions, conditional access, accurate sidewalks, and pedestrian area interiors are not modeled. The Java world still runs separately.

Local traffic preview: up to 14 cars and 24 walking figures spawned gradually at graph nodes 25–100 m from the player. Cars use directed motor edges; walkers use foot edges. Actors continue onto connected edges, yield approximately to nearby actors/player, and unload beyond 180 m. At a directed dead end an actor stops; there is no illegal reverse or visible teleport. Models are simple procedural geometry. This is ambient client-side traffic, not persistent residents, real driving physics, correct intersection right-of-way, or Java-controlled vehicles. Lane offsets are approximations and may clip narrow streets; actual building collision for traffic and sidewalks are not yet implemented.

Geographic population prototype: the default launcher starts a separate local Java server on 127.0.0.1:8091, with 500 deterministic fictional adults (experimental maximum 2000). Python exports public walk edges and building-to-nearest-street anchors, and Java chooses homes, shared households, jobs, approximate car ownership and work shifts. Routes use Dijkstra on the largest connected pedestrian component. Only commuting residents within 140 m are returned (max 40), with stable IDs, position and activity; no names above heads. At home/work they are not rendered. Godot replaces random pedestrians with these actors while the server is connected. The clock advances one game minute per five real seconds and persists every 15 seconds/on orderly close; roster is deterministically regenerated from the stable graph and population. The save is data/vigevano-world.properties, separate from world.db; changing map graph or count deliberately rejects the existing geographic save rather than silently overwriting it. Use bash play.sh --map --preview-only for standalone geometry/ambient traffic. Households are only shared-home groupings, not simulated family relationships. Workplaces are fictional assignments to mapped building anchors, not verified business locations; routes are pedestrian only, and car ownership does not yet control actual vehicles. No old Java economy/conversation system migration yet. All residents go to work daily; no weekends, fatigue, wages, births, or deaths in this prototype.

## Shared Java world (supersedes independent GeoWorld launcher)
The default launcher starts the original Spring GameSession on port 8091 and uses the SAME data/world.db as play.sh. Old identities, economy, police, health, families, jobs and contacts are retained. Existing legacy district IDs are projected to provisional walking-network anchors; these are not verified business locations. The original Java travel countdown is interpolated on connected OSM paths; private and indoor NPCs are excluded. E at a nearby marker enters the logical district without charging travel twice; T near a talkable resident opens Italian text chat through the original engine. Java advances on nearby requests, pausing for rebirth. The previous independent 500-person roster and properties file remain untouched but are not migrated. Population is now the original simulation default of 160 generated residents plus core people, not 500. Most original action menus, vehicles, and custody/rebirth interaction remain in play.sh. Ambient cars remain client-side; player physical coordinates are not persisted. The geographic projection is provisional, not a full migration. Do not run the two launchers concurrently.

### Shared action menu
F opens contextual actions provided by Java. Consumables and rest use the original player, and business/transport actions require being within 18 m of the saved district anchor. Existing shop stock, wages, skill requirements, work orders, fuel, vehicle ownership, bike parking, bus fares and travel rules remain authoritative. Car and bus trips are abstract original-engine actions followed by confirmed relocation; they are not manual driving or animated rides on the OSM graph. Parked personal vehicles are shown by simple models at provisional anchors; ambient traffic remains visual. Cycling changes movement speed but does not implement bicycle physics. No extra world database is created.

### Manual road-constrained driving and fictional vehicle theft
G near your parked car enters it; G near a marked civilian car takes it as a fictional offense. W advances, A/D select the next turn, Space stops acceleration, G exits. Java uses the directed OSM motor graph, limits the speed and fuel/condition, and confirms positions. This is graph-constrained driving (not free steering, suspension, collision simulation or a chase). Civilian cars are provisional parked fixtures in selected motor districts, not individual NPC-owned vehicles. Stolen vehicles have separate fuel/condition and do not replace owned cars; theft is reported, custody and court use the existing justice system, and the car is recovered at arrest or abandonment. On exit the player and owned vehicle park at the nearest logical district anchor. Current road-edge pose is transient, while district, fuel, condition and offense persist. Ambient traffic is still visual. Theft recovery on abrupt shutdown may need improvement.

Fuel distance remainders persist between trips. On detention the server clears active driving and the UI shows the report countdown. Ordinary district, dialogue and action commands are rejected during manual driving. A disconnected client freezes rather than independently moving the car.

### Resident ownership and police patrol rendering
A deterministic subset of living adult civilian NPCs owns an individually identified compact car (based on their permanent generation index). In the current logical district, the nearest selected owner's car is displayed when the resident is present and not traveling; it is not available while stolen. The recorded owner is kept in SQLite-backed justice state and the theft produces a resident event. Recovery clears that temporary possession; the owner's car becomes available again. This models ownership but not individual vehicle purchase, exact parking stalls, independent NPC driving schedules or persistent damage. Police patrol models are derived from scheduled living officers, limited to six nearby, projected to nearby motor nodes; their lights flash while a police report is pending. This is a visual projection of police duty, not yet a physical pursuit or a change to the existing detention logic.

### Geographic dispatch and contact damage
A reported offense can dispatch a currently scheduled, living officer on the directed motor graph. Java updates that response on nearby requests, projects it as a patrol vehicle, and uses the existing justice transition when it reaches the player. If no route or officer is available, the old timed justice mechanism remains active. This is a simplified game dispatch, not a traffic-law or real-world police simulation. Manual driving checks swept contact with the projected parked civilian vehicle (or the player's parked legal car while in a stolen car), applies an arcade condition loss with a cooldown, persists the updated condition and flashes the UI. It does not yet implement rigid-body impacts, body deformation, pedestrian collisions, or contact with every ambient car/building. Dispatch path position is transient; the criminal report and vehicle condition remain persisted.

### Resident transport economy
The first geographic clock tick enables the persisted resident transport system for the shared world. Existing deterministic adult civilian owners receive an initial car; other adults can buy one at the workshop if they can afford it while keeping cash for essentials. The Java population tick chooses car, bus or walking at departure; fuel/condition and parking district are saved, and workshop purchases, service and fuel use existing accounts and finite stock. Fuel and repair are simplified local deliveries to the parked vehicle, not physical service-station visits. Bus fares are paid on eligible logical routes, but bus capacity, timetable waiting and fleet scheduling are not yet simulated for NPCs. The original logical travel duration is unchanged. Godot removes random traffic in connected mode, projects up to 32 nearby resident vehicle trips on directed motor routes, and falls back to the old pedestrian visualization where no directed route exists. The bus visualization groups matching current logical trips; it is not a persistent physical bus fleet. Theft uses the actual resident fuel/condition and recovery returns the car to its original parking district. Exact parking spaces and continuous collision physics are still pending.

### Modular public interiors
E at a district marker now enters a modular public interior for market, bakery, cafe, school, clinic, workshop, library, office or factory. E again exits. The Java session owns the current room (transient), projects up to 24 present public-space residents into it, hides them from outdoor snapshots, and excludes private homes and residents in transit. Conversation and F actions use the original Java engine; transport actions are disallowed indoors. Godot builds a separate collision-bounded furnished room above the exterior and renders actual present residents. Room positions are arranged in a grid, not individual workstation animations, and physical rooms/doors are not yet attached to actual OSM building footprints. Reload resumes outside at the saved district. Private apartments and building opening/closing access rules are not implemented; existing business-action hours remain enforced.

### Interior workstations and observable activity
Java assigns working residents to six staff stations and up to twelve studying children to classroom desks; remaining capacity is for standing visitors. It reuses the original GameSession workVisual, outfitVisual and appearanceStage fields, not a new profession or identity database. Godot renders workstation furniture, a board, individual school desks/chairs, coarse body sizes, work clothing, held props and simple arm/leg animations based on the authoritative activity. Exit requires approaching the interior door. These are visual representations of the existing simulation; actual lesson content, medical examinations, retail transactions between specific residents and individualized work-order animation are not newly simulated. Public rooms remain modular, with no exact OSM facade attachment, and staff/seat capacity is limited.

### Private housing
At the north/south residential anchors, F lists existing rent/buy actions and known-address intercom actions. E only synchronizes the district; it does not unlock private property. Enter your own valid leased room or purchased house via F; use the same saved pantry, cooking, washing and sheltered sleep as the regular game. An invited apartment uses the existing intercom permission, availability, trust and time limits; only the host and available household members are projected, and conversation uses the original private-space validation. Guest actions include coffee, snack, conversation, dinner and rest, subject to original requirements. E at the door leaves and clears the visit. Expired invitations/leases close the interior. Godot builds a modular bedroom, kitchen, living and bathroom layout. Existing intercom only supports actual apartment entry at building 1 (other listed addresses allow doorstep visits); unlisted outer homes remain inaccessible. The room layout is generic, not a real Vigevano apartment or a fully individual floor plan. Furniture is fixed; domestic activity poses are simplified. Private room session itself is transient and is not saved as a new world.

### Batched exterior architecture
Procedural facade colors, windows, door surrounds, shutters and occasional balconies follow the imported building footprints and heights. Detail meshes are batched by material within each streamed chunk; original collision walls remain separate. A chunk is capped at 2200 windows and 16 street lamps. Pavements use OSM sidewalk tags when present and a provisional width heuristic otherwise; road marking strips and lamp locations are approximations. Trees are imported only from OSM nodes tagged natural=tree; cached extracts without those nodes need an optional --refresh to acquire them. The launcher automatically rebuilds older schema-2 JSON from the cached XML for detail_version=1. Day/night sky, directional light and emissive lamp materials follow the Java clock. Lamps do not cast individual local pools of light yet; there are no real facade photographs, precise balconies, weather effects or collision on decorative details. Large city performance and visual appearance require local testing.

### Local lighting and weather
The geographic snapshot now returns the same deterministic Java WeatherLife conditions as the main world. Godot displays temperature, seasonal foliage tint, overcast light, rain or snow via a bounded 240-instance visual volume around the player; precipitation is hidden inside modular interiors. At night a pool of at most eight nearest street lights illuminates the scene (no shadows). OSM bench nodes are included in detail_version=2; use --refresh to fetch new bench/tree tags if the cached XML predates those queries. Bench orientation and lamp locations are procedural approximations. Snow accumulation, puddles, wet-road friction, actual real-world weather and individualized weather-driven NPC animations are not implemented. Real-map performance is not yet benchmarked.

### Smartphone
P opens a modular phone UI with six apps: calls, messages, contacts, jobs, bank and meetings. Calls, reply buttons and invitations delegate to the existing PhoneLife actions with server-side availability checks; known addresses and numbers are never generated by Godot. Incoming calls appear in the outdoor HUD. Jobs display the actual public offers, skill requirements and payroll availability, and can set a walking route to the projected workplace. Bank displays the existing savings/business/debt balances and directions to Offices; financial transactions are still subject to the original in-person rules and are not added to this phone. The phone uses the same Java clock and SQLite state. It is unavailable during driving, custody or rebirth; free-form SMS, voice calls, app localization and bank-counter integration in geographic mode remain incomplete.

### Expanded real-road region (experimental)
Run `bash play.sh --wide` for a separate approximately 6.7 × 7.8 km OSM bounding box around Vigevano. Use `--wide --preview-only` to inspect geometry without touching the shared simulation. First launch downloads a larger extract once; Overpass availability and local RAM/performance may vary. Central and wide XML/JSON/graph caches are separate; both use the same meter-scale origin. The shared game still uses data/world.db and provisional district anchors, which may shift on a different extract. Back up the save before trying shared wide mode. Do not run launchers concurrently. Detail version 3 retains surface/junction tags, uses tagged widths or lane estimates, includes connecting road ramps, and distinguishes stone and unpaved roads by material color. Flat ground covers the imported graph. Real elevation, waterways, bridges/tunnels, full traffic rules and free-steering physics are not implemented here; this region is not the whole municipality. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.

### Waterways and vegetation (detail version 4)
Use `bash play.sh --wide --refresh --preview-only` once to fetch the added OSM waterway and tree-row tags. Cached older XML still converts and launches, but cannot contain features that were never downloaded. Open canals, rivers and streams are drawn as flat visual ribbons using mapped width or an estimate; underground/covered waterways are omitted. Tree rows are sampled every eight meters and the batched crowns use rounded low-poly geometry. Water is visual only: no river banks, water collision, swimming, flood physics, polygon riverbanks or elevation-aware bridges yet. Roads remain above the water ribbons at crossings. No real terrain elevation has been added.

### Driving visuals and NVIDIA launch defaults
PRIME offload is controlled by the caller: use prime-run bash play.sh --wide. The launcher never installs packages. The bundled CC0 vehicle models are now used for geographic ambient and civilian cars; police and the first-person driving body keep the existing procedural geometry to avoid clipping and beacon placement regressions. Imported model paints are authored by the asset, not dynamically repainted. Asphalt uses a world-space procedural grain shader, and major highways gain simple edge markings. No new driving physics or mountain elevation in this change. Compatibility stays the default renderer; local NVIDIA driver support and actual frame rate must be checked on the user's hardware.

### Weather and driving atmosphere
The geographic scene now uses a procedural day/night sky and rain-darkened asphalt, with two bounded shadowless headlights on the first-person driving body in darkness/rain. The original game also shares weather-sensitive asphalt materials across streamed districts. These are visual effects, not puddle simulation, true reflections, changing tire grip or new driving physics. The GPU cost is limited by two player lights; actual rendered quality and FPS still need hardware testing.

### First-person cabin
The geographic driving camera now sits on the left inside a lightweight dashboard and open windshield, with a turning steering wheel, a speed estimate from visual motion, and limited mouse head-look. Mirror surfaces are decorative; there are no live rear-view reflections. Heading is interpolated visually between server poses. Java still validates each road segment and controls movement; this is not free-steering or a new speed/physics model.

### Driving speed and cabin sound
The geographic server now maintains a bounded speed (maximum 36 km/h), acceleration, coasting and braking; W accelerates and Space/S brakes. Server responses expose speedKmh. A long pause resets speed and bounds movement, and dead ends/collisions stop the speed model. Both scenes use a lightweight synthesized engine and tire loop with pitch/volume related to speed; it is not a recording of a real vehicle. The older scene retains its own driving mechanics. No gear ratios, reverse in geographic mode, grip or suspension physics yet.

### Road-dependent speed targets (detail version 5)
Numeric OSM maxspeed and directional tags are carried through JSON and TSV to Java. Missing or symbolic tags use conservative road-class estimates. The game caps speed at 72 km/h; older TSV rows without a speed retain 36 km/h. Speed targets are not an authoritative traffic-law database, and deceleration at a road change is simplified. The dashboard shows simulated automatic gear bands D1–D5 with corresponding engine pitch changes, not physical gear ratios. Existing cached OSM can be reconverted locally. Fuel/wear balance, reversing, terrain and free steering are unchanged.

### Countryside land cover (detail version 6)
Run `prime-run bash play.sh --wide --refresh --preview-only` once to download land-use ways. Closed OSM farmland, meadow, woodland, orchard, vineyard, park and water outlines are indexed by 128 m chunks and clipped/triangulated at runtime. Fields use procedural crop rows; forests and orchards have up to 64 decorative trees per chunk with approximate clearance from nearby mapped roads, rails, waterways and building footprints. Multipolygon relations and holes, actual elevation, crops as economic resources, collision for vegetation/water, and true bridges are not implemented. Cached old XML lacks newly requested land-use features until refreshed. Shapes come from OSM, surface textures and tree positions are approximations.

### Bridge details and distant horizon (detail version 7)
Mapped bridge tags now produce concrete edge strips and metal parapets; the existing road centerline stays flat. They are visual only, not collision barriers or elevated decks. A lightweight three-layer procedural mountain backdrop faces the northern/western horizon, changes with daylight and overcast sky, and is hidden indoors. It is not a DEM-derived panorama or a drivable mountain area. No terrain elevations or serpentines have been added yet. Existing cached OSM can be reconverted without fetching new tags.

### Textured buildings and street names
The geographic renderer shares five original mipmapped procedural textures for plaster, clay roof tiles, stone paving, wood and grass. Roof finishes are inferred from building type, not surveyed roof shape/material. Up to four decorative two-sided street-name plates per streamed tile use real OSM names but approximate locations, no regulatory meaning or collision. Geometry and map cache schema are unchanged. No new assets need downloading. Visual quality and frame rate still need local testing.

### Gabled roofs and shop fronts
Small nearly rectangular residential footprints receive a procedural raised ridge and gable walls. Irregular/large polygons retain their existing roof triangulation. Explicit retail/commercial buildings receive decorative glazed storefronts, frames and awnings on a qualifying first facade; the glazing changes brightness with daylight. Neither building shape nor window placement is a surveyed reconstruction; roof height is estimated above the existing wall height. Shopfronts are opaque scenery, not new accessible shops or actual opening hours. No new collision or assets, traffic signals or intersections in this commit.

### Traffic controls (detail version 8)
Refresh XML once to retrieve mapped traffic signal/crossing nodes. Signals attached to incoming motor edges show red, amber and green using a shared 34-second local-clock cycle; player Java movement and ambient preview cars stop before red/amber. Marked crossings draw simple zebra stripes. Timing and approach layout are approximations, not actual municipal programs; unmarked crossings are skipped. Shared Java commuter and patrol schedules do not yet obey the signal controller, and pedestrians have no separate crossing state. No traffic-law penalties or new collision barriers. Existing old maps remain compatible but require refresh to retrieve new nodes.

### Shared commuter route rendering
Java traffic snapshots now include the directed route polyline, trip key and target distance. Godot advances visible civilian cars and buses along that polyline instead of taking straight-line shortcuts between snapshots, stops them at mapped red/amber signals and applies a simple forward queue gap. This is a visual traffic layer: Java still owns travel schedules and may complete a trip before its visual car arrives; vehicles can disappear when the authoritative trip ends or leaves the visible radius. Police and pedestrian routing, actual lane reservation, collision physics and signal-aware schedule delays are not yet implemented. Route data increases snapshot size; real-map performance needs testing.

### Stealable municipal reserve bus
In shared geographic mode, enter the station district and approach the parked reserve bus, then press G. The Java server verifies district and proximity, records municipal vehicle theft using the existing justice system, and keeps stolen fuel/condition separate from the player's own car. The bus has lower acceleration and braking, a 54 km/h maximum, a scaled driver cabin and a new exterior used by commuter buses. On exit or police detention the reserve is automatically recovered; municipal reserve servicing is abstract (fuel and condition reset on the next theft). Only the parked reserve is stealable, not occupied commuter buses. Its bay is approximate and the existing scheduled bus service is independent. No real-world theft technique or passenger interaction is simulated. No new data download required.

### Freight reserve truck and reserve vehicle condition
A parked freight reserve truck at the mapped workshop district can be entered with G. It uses a separate truck model with three axles, a larger cabin, a 60 km/h cap and reduced acceleration/braking. Theft uses the same Java justice flow. Bus and truck reserve fuel/condition are retained in world stocks after recovery, independent of each other and the player; they no longer refill magically. No maintenance schedule for these reserve vehicles yet, so a depleted reserve is unavailable until a later maintenance feature. This is not the existing service-van fleet or freight job inventory. Parking and recovery remain abstract; no moving occupied vehicles or cargo loading yet.

### Paid reserve maintenance
Recovered municipal bus and freight reserve truck are now serviced at 08:00 world time, at most once per day each, from the existing city-maintenance account (both reserves treated as municipal assets for now). Repairs below 70 condition use one spare part and €15 for up to 30 condition; refueling purchases up to six units, limited by actual station stock, tank capacity and funds. A stolen vehicle in player custody cannot be serviced by its owner. No changes to unused reserve assets, private logistics-company trucks or personal vehicles. Expense counters are persisted in world stocks; there is no new maintenance animation or budget UI. Lack of funds or supplies can still leave reserves unusable.

### Two-wheel reserve transport
The market district has a municipal scooter and the park district a reserve motorcycle, with approximate parking bays. G enters the nearest available parked reserve under the existing theft/justice rules. Scooter target is capped at 45 km/h, motorcycle at 72 km/h; acceleration differs. First-person two-wheel model has handlebars, mirrors, speed display and a cosmetic lean. Fuel capacities are 6 and 9 units respectively, with paid reserve servicing after recovery. No licensed purchase, NPC riders, functional mirrors, separate headlamp lighting, balancing simulation, or road-class restrictions specific to mopeds yet. Both use the directed motor graph and existing traffic signals.

### Personal two-wheel vehicle shop
In geographic visitor mode, approach the workshop during 08:00–18:00 and open F actions. Buy a used scooter for €75 or a motorcycle for €140, one of each in addition to a car. Use G near the owned vehicle to ride without theft. Sale price is condition-dependent and requires dealer funds. Refueling consumes shared station fuel and personal money; service costs €10 and one spare part. Ownership, district parking, fuel, wear and distance remainder persist in incarnation-scoped world stocks. Parking positions are approximate, not exact saved coordinates. Linked/rebirth characters, inheritance, insurance and original non-geographic mode are not supported for this new shop yet. Existing reserve theft behavior remains separate.

### Driving school and licenses
Geographic visitor mode has fictional AM (scooter), A (motorcycle), B (car) qualifications. Visit the school during 08:00–18:00 for two paid theory lessons (€5 each) and a three-question quiz; all answers must be correct. Failed quizzes can be retried. Then visit the workshop for two supervised practice sessions (€8 each, time-based simulation, not interactive driving tests), and the municipal office for issuance (€4). License progress is persisted separately per incarnation and category. A/B cover scooter in this simplified game model. The HUD displays licensed categories. Starting an owned vehicle without the appropriate license produces a warning, but remains possible; no automatic traffic fines or license expiry yet. Theft behavior is unchanged. No linked-life or non-geographic integration yet. Rules/fees are fictional, not Italian law.

### Heavy licenses and fictional registration plates
C (truck) and D (bus) use the same €30 theory, quiz, practice and issuance sequence as B, with B as a prerequisite. These are game qualifications, not real legal rules. Java exposes stable fictional registration numbers for personal car/two-wheel types (per incarnation), resident cars (per generation index), and municipal reserve vehicles. Godot renders rear plates; commuter buses are still abstract trips rather than individually registered fleet vehicles. Numbers identify vehicle slots, not a full transferable title registry; selling and repurchasing a personal vehicle of the same type retains its slot number. Plate mesh placement on imported cars is approximate. No new enforcement, insurance, actual driving exam or police registry lookup yet.

### Roadside document checks
In geographic visitor driving, moving a personal car or two-wheeler within 12 m of a non-responding on-duty mapped patrol can trigger a check, at most once per 30 game minutes per incarnation. The car stops for four real seconds. Missing license category incurs a fictional €35 fine; condition below 25 incurs €20. A legal driver with acceptable condition is cleared. Fines are persistent administrative debt, not criminal convictions; use F at municipal office 08:00–17:00 to pay all or part, credited to city-maintenance. Existing stolen-vehicle dispatch remains unchanged. No pull-over lane, NPC dialogue, insurance, inspection certificate or stolen-plate database yet; patrol detection is proximity-based and the fine amounts are fictional.

### Personal vehicle documents
For each owned car, scooter and motorcycle, buy a fictional 30-day insurance certificate at the municipal office (car €20, motorcycle €15, scooter €10), and a 60-day inspection at the workshop (€10, vehicle parked there and condition at least 60). Renewals are offered in the final seven days or after expiration. Existing vehicles start without certificates; sale or repurchase of a two-wheeler clears its documents. The HUD lists remaining game days. Roadside checks add €25 for missing insurance and €15 for missing inspection to the existing fines. Insurance premium goes to a dedicated insurer account; claim payouts and accident liability are not implemented yet. These are game mechanics, not actual Italian requirements. No linked-life integration.

### Collision injuries and limited insurance payouts
Existing geographic swept contact with parked vehicles now scales vehicle damage (1–35) and driver injury (0–30) with speed; two-wheel riders are more vulnerable. This first pass is nonfatal (health stays at least 1 for a living driver). Personal insured vehicles with the correct license receive limited own-damage compensation (2 EUR per actual collision damage point minus 5 EUR deductible, max 25 EUR), capped by insurer cash; stolen vehicles receive none. The injury/claim totals persist per incarnation. F at clinic offers 30-minute injury care for €8, recovering up to 15 health within recorded injury balance. No moving-vehicle collision detection, third-party liability, tow truck, ambulance, real injury diagnosis or fatal crashes yet. Existing district parking/contact approximations remain; new mechanics are fictional.

### Paid recovery service
In geographic visitor mode, leave a private car/scooter/motorcycle that is out of fuel or below condition 3, then use F near its district anchor to request a tow (06:00–22:00). One job per incarnation at a time. The fee is €12 plus €2 per reserved fuel unit (1–6 units), consumed from shared station stock; travel takes 17–90 game minutes based on district distance. The workshop receives the service portion. At the due world tick only the vehicle is delivered to the workshop, with unchanged fuel and condition; the player remains where they are. Pending vehicles cannot start manual geographic driving or be serviced/sold through their shop actions. The HUD shows remaining time. This is a persisted timed service, not yet a visible tow truck, actual route or staff assignment; no phone call, stolen vehicles or linked-life support. Fees and timing are fictional.

### Ambulance dispatch and stabilization
In geographic visitor mode, a living road-accident patient with health at or below 80 and untreated recorded injuries can request an ambulance via F near the district marker or the Calls tab of an available in-game phone. The municipal account pays the reserved fuel and €8 care fee. Dispatch takes 6–45 game minutes; F to board after arrival advances travel time and places the patient at the clinic, leaving the vehicle behind, and restores up to 15 injury health. No player charge. Leaving the district or waiting more than 30 game minutes after arrival cancels the request and refunds unused care, not fuel. The HUD displays status. Funds or fuel shortages can block dispatch. No visible ambulance model, paramedic staff assignment, hospital bed admission, real-world emergency call or linked-life support yet. Game rules are fictional.

### Visible ambulance dispatch
An active ambulance call now projects an emergency vehicle from the clinic to the pickup district over the existing directed motor route. Position follows the authoritative dispatch clock and the existing Godot route interpolation; the ambulance is prioritized in the nearby traffic limit and remains at the destination road node until boarding or cancellation. It has a procedural white/orange van body, medical markings, rear number LA EMS1, alternating emissive blue beacons and a small local blue light. If no connected motor route exists, the visual vehicle is omitted while the abstract service remains available. It uses the current traffic signal/queue interpolation, not a special right-of-way or collision system. At logical arrival its visual position is synchronized to the destination; boarding still uses district-level travel, not a physical interior or paramedic NPC. No audible siren or return-trip visualization yet.

### Spatial emergency sirens
Dispatching ambulances and responding police patrols now emit distinct original synthesized looping tones via AudioStreamPlayer3D. The sound follows the vehicle with distance attenuation and a 160 m maximum audible distance (within the current streamed vehicle radius). Ambulance siren stops on logical arrival; police siren stops when response ends. Audio streams are cached and removed with their vehicles. No external audio downloads, real siren recordings, acoustic occlusion or new yielding/lane-change logic; current signal and traffic queue behavior is unchanged. Actual audio output and volume balance still need in-game listening.

### Fire service
Geographic transport-enabled worlds can generate a fictional district incident every fifth world day at 13:00, rotating among four districts. A visitor in that district may report it with F (phone action is exposed by Java but not yet rendered in the Calls tab); an abstract resident reports after ten minutes. Dispatch waits for municipal money, fuel and four water units (treated water when the network is active, otherwise the public tank). A red fire engine with roof ladder, blue beacons and distinct spatial siren follows the mapped route from a provisional workshop depot. On arrival, extinguishing takes 15 game minutes. If resources are unavailable for 120 minutes, the incident ends uncontrolled; damage is a cumulative abstract counter, not yet physical building damage or injuries. One incident at a time, no fire particles, dedicated station building, firefighter NPC staffing or physical firefighting yet. The HUD lists district, state and time; missing road routes suppress only the visual engine. Fictional simulation, not real emergency guidance.

### Visible fire and phone reporting
The geographic client receives a district fire indicator with stable incident identity and intensity. It renders a bounded cluster of seven orange emissive flame meshes, twelve rising translucent smoke puffs and a flickering non-shadowed light; intensity grows before response and decreases during suppression. The effect is removed when the incident clears, when indoors/disconnected or beyond 280 m. Placement is a provisional public district offset, not a selected building footprint; no destructive mesh edits or physical spread. ### Firefighter staffing

### Resident evacuation
During an active fire, autonomous generated residents in the affected district or scheduled to enter it receive a persistent incident marker and a new destination at the park. The HUD displays affected and sheltered counts; cumulative evacuation entries count each resident once per incident. Assigned firefighters are excluded; player-controlled linked characters and original special residents are not forcibly moved. Firefighters on scene use a distinct activity visible outdoors. This is district-level shelter routing, not physical room evacuation, crowd collision, or accommodation logistics. Existing unresolved fires still clear under the original timeout rule.

### Persistent fire recovery
Each affected district has persistent condition (100 by default). Fire waiting/response time reduces condition by four points per five-minute tick; an uncontrolled incident adds 20 damage. Active fire or condition below 70 closes the affected district's normal interior entry and basic retail actions, and blocks factory-network production there. Generated residents avoid closed destinations; displaced northern/southern residential NPCs use the park until condition reaches 70. At 14:00 an available workshop carpenter can carry out one abstract municipal restoration per day, prioritizing the worst damaged district with available resources. Qualified visitors (engineering 3) may choose a 60-minute restoration at the district via F. Each restores up to 20 condition, consumes one existing building-material unit, transfers €4 to the supplier and €6 to the worker from the municipal budget. No material or money is created. HUD shows condition and closure. This is a district-level model, not individual property ownership, insurance, physical structural collapse, individual shelter beds or all possible commerce/industrial endpoints. NPC repair is abstract, no animated construction crew yet.

### Home fire insurance

### Business liquidity and wage arrears
In staffed worlds the market vendor, core factory machinist and factory-network occupations draw hourly pay from their actual business accounts (market/network €4, core machinist €11). Partial pay records the shortfall per worker and account, once per hour. The daily 06:30 review settles surviving workers' outstanding wages, even after job loss, and records consecutive days of insufficient funds or remaining arrears. After three distressed reviews the firm suspends and procedural staff lose their jobs through the existing employment/social protection path. Staffing and normal job applications reject suspended employers; network production and paid visitor shifts are blocked. If outstanding wages are paid and enough cash remains for an hourly wage, suspension lifts at the next review; hiring follows existing systems. HUD reports combined arrears and suspensions. Legacy small worlds retain old payroll behavior. This is liquidity suspension, not legal bankruptcy or asset liquidation; market sales can continue from existing stock, and not all business types are covered. Debt of deceased workers is not settled or inherited yet.

### Business credit
The existing town bank lends at most one recovery loan per day at 10:00, requiring an available bank clerk. In staffed worlds a firm with at least two distressed daily reviews and no open business loan may receive just enough to cover recorded wage arrears plus a €40 working reserve (maximum €100, no partial underfunded loan). Lending uses existing free bank cash and leaves deposits plus €100 bank reserve protected. Arrears are settled immediately; existing daily solvency review controls reopening. Weekly interest is 4% rounded up in whole euros, with outstanding balance capped at €150. From the following day, a €10 daily installment is collected only from surplus above €40 and after wage arrears are settled. Partial or missed installments are tracked; no cash is minted. Office HUD reports balances and overdue firms. No formal collateral, underwriting of future revenues, seizure, bankruptcy auction or manual player business loan yet. This is a fictional game mechanic.
